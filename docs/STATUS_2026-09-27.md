## What the headline claim looks like now

attached. It reads 0.752 on GPU and 0.730 on CPU; the behavior-controlled signal reads
0.723 on GPU (seven-channel basis) and 0.710 on CPU. The film's 73% is the GPU

misses the bar. If the device shift is similar there, CPU accounts for about 0.02 of
that instance's 0.113 gap to the headline.

L3 gate's detector noise the L2 artifacts read 0.646 and 0.618 against L3's 0.928. The
matched "detectable but not encoded" contrast is L1 (oracle 0.873, survival 0.533)
against L3.

## Open gaps, in the order I would work them

1. The cross-validation split depends on the software stack. `grouped_auroc`
   (`itasorl/experiment_a.py`) uses `GroupKFold` with one group per episode, and older
   scikit-learn orders equal-sized groups with an unstable `np.argsort`, so fold
   membership depends on the numpy version and probably the CPU. The published GPU
   numbers are exact fold means under the split (24,20), (22,22), (21,23), (21,23),
   (22,22); all three cloud runs sit on balanced 22/22 folds. Both arms of the step 2
   comparison now share one split, so that comparison is clean. The published numbers
   still do not reproduce bit-exactly on current scikit-learn, and
   `audit_stats_recheck.py` compares docs with artifacts, so it cannot catch this. A
   simulation at AUROC near 0.73 (300 synthetic datasets, not project data) puts the
   shift at about 0.011 per seed and 0.004 on a 10-seed mean. Fix: seeded explicit
   folds, pinned scikit-learn and numpy, then re-score the saved GPU dumps under both
   splits.

2. Step 2 does not fully separate the decoder from survival skill. On the same device
   the decoder-carrying agents return -0.355 at train@0.45 eval@0.45 against -0.474
   without it. That gap is 0.12, down from 0.26 against the GPU run, and FINDINGS 10.8
   now states it. A skill-matched model-free run would close it: train the model-free
   arm until its return reaches the decoder agent's, then probe.

3. Step 4 changed seed and capacity together. G seed 2 at hidden 8 separates them.

4. Oracle AUROC does not fix what the organism picks up: G seed 1 at hidden 8 matches
   the headline oracle (0.928) with a dirty floor (0.664). FINDINGS 10.9 records it.

5. The cloud runs keep only per-seed cells, results, and behavior-audit JSON. Their
   state dumps and saved agents are gone, so readout-only follow-ups on those agents
   need retraining, about four hours of CPU each.

6. The sensory-echo control has run only at hidden 8. Hidden 7, where the behavior
   control was least clean, is readout-only on saved agents. (Probe B is the nonlinear
   joint control at hidden 8, a different check.)
