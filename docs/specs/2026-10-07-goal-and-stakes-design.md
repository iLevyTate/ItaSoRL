# Goal and stakes: does the revealing walk need survival?

Date: 2026-10-07
Status: frozen before any run (no cell of `T-touch`, `S-immortal` or `S-scarce` existed when
this was committed; the scarce rung's food setting is fixed by the calibration rule below and
recorded as a dated amendment before launch)
Plan: follows `docs/REVISION_2026-10.md`. Register: `docs/PREREGISTRATION_L3.md` section 12,
entry dated 2026-10-07.

## Purpose

The 2026-10 revision withdrew "survival-specific". On the survival agent's own streams a
trunk trained only to predict reads the condition as well as the survival trunk (FINDINGS
17.6: 0.721 and 0.738 against 0.733), and a decoder with no access to any agent reads the
survival agent's observation stream at 0.724 (17.8). What carries the signal is the
trajectories the survival policy generates. Two questions are left open by that record:

1. Does the walk need survival, or does any goal that sends the body to the same targets
   produce it?
2. Does the reading change when dying is a real possibility? At the registered food density
   no readout pool recorded a death, and a random walker survives most of an episode.

The runs below answer each with the corrected trainer, cell for cell against `C1`.

## What the record already shows

Stream-only decoders and trunk readings on the `C1` agents at drift 0.45 (FINDINGS 17.6, 17.8):

| walks generated by | decoder on the observation stream | trunk reading |
|---|---|---|
| survival policy | 0.724 [0.670, 0.778] | 0.733 [0.669, 0.797] |
| predictor policy | 0.602 [0.579, 0.625] | 0.589 [0.567, 0.610] |
| untrained policy | 0.583 [0.563, 0.602] | 0.523 [0.491, 0.554] |
| one scripted random walk | not run | 0.551 to 0.577, every arm |

The survival policy's walks reveal the condition and the others' mostly do not. The missing
cell, a stream-only decoder on the scripted walk, is added to the readouts below.

## Conditions

Every run uses the `C1` protocol unchanged
(`docs/specs/2026-10-06-corrected-trainer-confirmation-design.md`): drift-mode l3,
l3-hidden 8, G seed 0, drifts [0.0, 0.45], agent seeds 0..9, updates 300, n_eps 16,
max_steps 80, hidden 96, ray_steps 5, shaping 1.0, pooled n = 110 by 24 steps, matched
pairs 60/20/24, successor bootstrap, auxiliary on, `--save-agents`, `ITASORL_FOLDS=explicit`.
No budget extension: the headline arm is the 300-update agent and nothing else is trained.

| Run | Reward of the trained arm | Death | Food | Flags beyond the protocol |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `C1` (exists) | survival: intake minus cost | on | registered (24 pellets, basal 0.4) | none |
| `T-touch` | +1 per step on which a pellet was eaten; energy cost ignored | off | registered | `--objective touch --mortal off` |
| `S-immortal` | survival | off | registered | `--mortal off` |
| `S-scarce` | survival | on | scarce (calibrated below) | `--n_pellets <calibrated>` |

The predictor and untrained arms are objective-free. In `T-touch` and `S-immortal` they are
trained on the same scripted episodes as `C1` and must equal `C1`'s arms to the bit
(integrity check 2). In `S-scarce` the world differs, so they are retrained and reported.

## Code changes

Two flags on `scripts/run_expB2.py`, threaded through the per-cell config:

- `--objective {survival,touch}`, default `survival`. `touch` remaps the per-step training
  reward in the episode collector to 1.0 when the ecology step reports `ate`, else 0.0.
  The world, its observation, and the potential-based food shaping are untouched. The
  engagement gate scores the trained arm and the baselines on this same reward.
- `--mortal {on,off}`, default `on`. `off` sets one attribute on the world so the death
  check returns false; energy, hydration, and temperature still evolve and are still
  observed. Death is the only terminal event, so an immortal episode always runs to the
  cutoff and bootstraps from the successor state.

Both keys are dropped from the config fingerprint at their defaults, as `gae_bootstrap`
and `l3_family` are, so `C1`'s fingerprint and every historical checkpoint are unchanged
(`tests/test_goal_stakes.py` asserts this against the recorded `C1` fingerprint).

The scarce rung needs no code: `--n_pellets` and `--basal_e` already exist.

## Calibration of the scarce rung (frozen rule)

Basal burn stays at the registered 0.4. Sweep `n_pellets` over 24, 16, 12, 8, 6, 4 with 200
scripted-policy episodes of 80 steps per world per setting (drift 0 authentic, drift 0.45
surrogate), the random walker reported beside it. Choose the largest `n_pellets` at which
the scripted walker's 80-step death rate at drift 0 lies in [0.40, 0.60]. Require the drift
0.45 death rate to be within 0.10 of it, so the stakes are not themselves a world leak, and
zero deaths within the first 24 steps in both worlds, so the pooled readout window is
unaffected. If no setting lands in the band, raise basal burn to 0.5 and sweep once more;
if still none, `S-scarce` is not run and is reported as uncalibrated. The chosen setting
is recorded as a dated amendment before launch.

Why the 24-step window is safe: starting energy 1.0 at a basal burn of 0.4 per unit time
(0.02 per step at dt 0.05) exhausts in about 50 steps for an agent that never eats, so no
pellet count can produce a death inside the 24-step pooled readout. Deaths are expected in
training episodes and in the 80-step matched-pair channel, where they are counted and
reported; the matched-pair channel already handles survivorship asymmetry by design.

## Readouts per run

1. The standard primary readout and every registered gate (`decide_h_b2`).
2. The three policy protocols of 17.6 (`own`, `scripted`, `replay`) through
   `scripts/run_policy_controlled_readouts.py`.
3. The stream-only decoders of 17.8 on each arm's own episodes, plus the scripted walk.
4. Deaths and true return per world and per arm, from the training log and the
   matched-pair channel.
5. The value-of-world-information contrast of 17.11 (matched assignment minus the better
   single policy, `scripts/validate_population_readout.py`), reported per run.

Pools are regenerated from the saved agents, as 17.8 did; no state dumps are shipped.

## Integrity checks (before any comparison is read)

1. Every cell records `gae_bootstrap = successor`, and its `objective` and `mortal` values.
2. In `T-touch` and `S-immortal` the predictor and untrained pooled targets equal `C1`'s at
   the same (drift, seed) to the bit. A mismatch stops the comparison.
3. The fold partition is recorded.
4. In `S-scarce`, the recorded death rates match the calibration amendment within 0.10.

## Decision rules (frozen)

Engagement for the touch objective: trained touch return at least 1.5 times the better of
the random and scripted baselines (the registered absolute margin of 0.15 was calibrated on
the survival reward's scale). Engagement for the survival rungs: the registered rule.

- **`T-touch`.** Meets the primary rule with every gate passing: "a pellet-directed goal
  without survival stakes produces walks from which the condition is decodable; survival is
  not required." Otherwise: "pellet-seeking without stakes did not meet the registered
  criterion."
- **Stakes.** The reading grows with the stakes only if the survival means order
  `S-immortal` < `C1` < `S-scarce` and the seed-paired `S-scarce` minus `S-immortal` contrast
  is at least +0.05 with its t-based 90% interval excluding zero. Then: "the reading grows
  with the stakes." Anything else: "within the tested range, stakes do not change the
  reading."
- **No wording about survival specificity** returns unless `T-touch` fails and the stakes
  rule is met, and then only as "pellet-seeking alone was not enough in this world".
- The value-of-world-information contrast is reported, not thresholded.
- A gate that fails or is not shown is reported as failed or open; margins are not relaxed.

## Compute and provenance

Each run executes in a Claude Code cloud session (the device class of `C1` and `C2`:
4 vCPU, torch CPU) on its own branch `run/<name>`, with `--out-dir artifacts/goal_stakes/<name>`
so cell checkpoints are tracked and committed as they land. A scheduled cloud routine
pulls the branch, launches the run with `--resume`, commits new cells, and exits; it skips a
firing when a lock file on the branch is younger than the longest observed cell time, and
retires itself when all 20 cells exist. Saved agents are attached to the run's pull request
as a release asset. Each run gets a results-manifest row with its commit, device string,
and the two new config keys. Order: `T-touch`, `S-immortal`, then `S-scarce` after its
calibration amendment.

## What this does not test

A surrogate that costs food only in the fake world (rejected: the agent observes its own
energy, so that would leak the condition into the observation). Whether the signal is
specific to learned dynamics (17.9). Retention under identical input (17.7).
