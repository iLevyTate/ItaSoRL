A tabula-rasa artificial-life experiment

When does a mind learn what it was never asked to care about?

ItaSoRL raises a digital organism from scratch inside a simulated world. Sometimes that world is authentic. Sometimes it is a subtly flawed copy. We never tell the organism, and never reward it for noticing: we read its internal state and ask when the difference gets encoded anyway.

01  /  The premise

A flaw in the world can be trivially detectable from the outside and still leave no trace inside the mind. That gap is the object of study.

The organism lives in "A Patch of Earth": a small, deterministic, Earth-like world. Sometimes it runs on authentic dynamics; sometimes on a surrogate carrying tiny substrate seams. Difficulty is dialed on a ladder from L0 (identical control) to L4 (adversarial).

Three invariants hold everywhere: tabula rasa (no imported semantics), readout not reward (world identity is probed, never trained), and substrate-grounded (the only tells are artifacts of the computation itself).

Detectability ceiling

Agent-free first: can a privileged detector spot the surrogate from the seams alone? Yes, and how well depends on the noise in its view, so every rung is also scored at one shared detector noise. Difficulty is calibrated and the confound audit is clean.

Incidental encoding

Does a prediction-only agent come to carry the flaw it was never asked to care about? Not at L2: the artifact a noiseless oracle catches almost perfectly leaves the state near chance across three readout channels, under the registered bar. Give that oracle the detector noise the L3 gate uses and it reads 0.62 to 0.65, so the matched version of this case is L1: oracle 0.873, survival agent 0.533 on the original trainer.

Foraging plus prediction

Against a learned-dynamics fingerprint, the state of agents trained to survive and to predict their next observation separates the worlds: {{c1_survival}} (90% CI {{c1_ci_lo}} to {{c1_ci_hi}}) with the trainer corrected in October 2026, against {{c1_predictor}} for prediction-only and {{c1_untrained}} untrained. Without the next-observation predictor the survival agent reads {{c2_survival}}, under the bar; the predictor adds {{aux_contrast}} (90% CI {{aux_ci_lo}} to {{aux_ci_hi}}) at 300 updates, and with more training the gap holds at about equal return. Then the check that changed the story: feed a prediction-only brain the exact senses the survival agent lived through and it reads the world within about 0.01 of the survival brain (0.721 against 0.733), and a decoder that never sees the brain reads 0.724 from the senses and actions alone. The signal rides on where the foraging policy takes the body, and a brain trained to predict keeps it. A hand-written drag that drifts the same coherent way is read too (0.754); white jitter of the same size is not. The common-garden control passes its frozen rule ({{cg_forward}} forward, {{cg_reverse}} reverse on the original agents): prefix condition remains decodable after restoring authentic dynamics, and on the corrected agents mostly through the body's footprint rather than memory (see FINDINGS 10.6.1 and 17). One honesty check stays open: with both worlds real, the survival state reads {{c1_l0}} on the registered pair of evaluation worlds, the largest of nine draws on record (the eight independent draws deviate from chance by 0.037 on average, and the gate accepts on two of them), so the registered rule as a whole is not met and the result is reported on its decodability clauses only. A balanced readout on the same world seeds reads 0.767, and carried to drift 0.45 on the agents of the historical hidden-8 run, every one of the eight independent draws clears the bar (0.728 to 0.860; the registered draw's 0.774 ranks 5 of 9), SECURE under a rule frozen before the run. Historical numbers on the original trainer: 0.752, and 0.730 against 0.601 without the predictor on the same CPU; an independently trained second fingerprint keeps the margin under the bar (0.639).

EXP C · done

Emergence under selection

Put the food where knowing the world pays, then breed lineages for 30 generations. Does world identity become more decodable? The contrast is flat, and the registered verdict is uninformative. Selection raised fitness in every run, yet one probe fit across the final foragers reads 0.509, chance, and the contrast against control lineages with food underfoot is -0.002 (90% CI -0.013 to +0.009). Scored one forager at a time, the individuals average 0.537 and none reaches the bar. The speed control is under its bar (gate 5), and the preregistration interprets no contrast unless every gate passes, so neither readout yields a verdict in either direction. The payoff for knowing the world (gate 1) is weak and falls short only of a draft margin that was never ratified (FINDINGS 13.F). A first pilot ran on code with two measurement bugs and was thrown out; these numbers come from fixed code. The L4 adversarial rung has not started.

03  /  The readout

Detectable is not the same as learned.

After training, a linear probe reads the agent's internal state and asks: is world identity in there? At L1 and L2 the answer stays near chance while an outside oracle catches the flaw. At L3 the readout lifts for agents trained to survive and to predict their next observation. Fed the same senses, a prediction-only brain reads the world within about 0.01 of the survival brain, so the signal rides on where the foraging policy goes (FINDINGS 17.6).

05  /  What's new here

Others told their AI to look for the fake, or used minds that already knew what "fake" means. We measure what a from-scratch mind comes to know unasked.

Evaluation awareness

Chatbots can tell a test from a real conversation, but they read the internet, so the concept of "being tested" was imported, not discovered. A student with the answer key. Our organism is born knowing nothing.

Incidental probing

Probing studies (AtariARI, Othello-GPT) found knowledge nobody taught, but always about things inside one fixed world: the pieces, the board, the score. Never about which world is running.

Reality-gap detectors

Robots get engineered alarms that fire when the world stops matching their training (GalilAI, RAPT): an installed smoke detector, detection as the designed objective. We install nothing and only read.

Meta-RL

The closest relative. RL², VariBAD, PEARL, and RMA train agents across many environments, so working out which one they are in pays, and their states come to track it. Ours train in one world each, no objective term targets the condition, and the condition is real law versus learned copy. Training across both worlds is the untested next step.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to measure the gap between external detectability and spontaneous internal encoding of simulator authenticity (authentic base dynamics vs a learned surrogate) in tabula-rasa recurrent agents, with substrate-level artifacts as the only cue and world identity read out post-hoc, never rewarded.

Film two  /  Two Minds

Same brain, two worlds, one flaw.

Now go inside the creature's head. One brain lives in the real world and in the copy. A title card, five chapters, and an end card follow the clue from the senses, through the processing layer, into the memory spine, and out to the motor heads, where the fake world's reach falls short. The flaw enters at speed, the sense the broken velocity law corrupts. Teal rings settle on the memory cells that end up holding it.

Names, rings and numbers are measured; the motion is illustrative. Every dot carries its real name from the observation spec. The twelve memory cells are real recurrent units of one brain from the corrected registered run, the seed whose survival reading sits closest to the ten-brain mean of {{c1_survival}}. Ring strength is each unit's measured world-signal, from that brain's pools rebuilt from its saved weights (within 0.002 of the recorded readings). The numbers on screen are means over ten brains: an untrained brain reads 52% ({{c1_untrained}}), a brain trained in the copy reads 73% ({{c1_survival}}), and a brain trained only to predict its senses, fed the same senses, reads 72%. The bar, 65%, was set in advance.

The memory chapter shows two different brains side by side, an untrained one and a trained one, each read once after training; no time course was measured. The end card carries the two caveats the record keeps. Without the second job, predicting what comes next, the reading falls under the bar at 300 training updates (61%) and clears it at 450 (69%). Two identical real worlds read 56% instead of 50% ({{c1_l0}}), so the preregistered test as a whole is not passed (FINDINGS 17).
